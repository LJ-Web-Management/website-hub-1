HAZWOPER, HazCom, RCRA or SPCC? A training map for plants

Plants answer to several overlapping training rules. Here's how they fit together.

A typical manufacturing plant juggles several training requirements that sound alike. Here's what each covers.

The map

RuleCoversWho
Hazard Communication (1910.1200)Chemical hazards at work, labels and SDSsEvery employee exposed to hazardous chemicals
HAZWOPER (q) (1910.120(q))Emergency response to releasesAnyone expected to respond; awareness for those who may discover a release
Emergency Action Plan (1910.38)Evacuation and alarmsEvery employee
RCRA generator training (40 CFR 262)Hazardous waste handlingHazardous waste personnel
SPCC (40 CFR 112.7(f))Oil spill preventionOil-handling personnel
DOT hazmat (49 CFR 172.704)Shipping hazmatAnyone who prepares or signs hazmat shipments

The common gap

Plants usually do HazCom and evacuation well. The gaps tend to be:

  • Operators who are informally expected to "deal with" leaks but have no (q) training
  • Waste handlers without documented RCRA training
  • The person who signs waste manifests without DOT training

A practical bundle

  • Everyone: HazCom, the emergency action plan and OSHA 4-Hour HAZWOPER First Responder Awareness Level Training
  • Waste handlers: RCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Training
  • Oil handlers: OSHA Spill Prevention and Response Safety Training
  • ERT and shift leads: Manufacturing Emergency Response Team (ERT) Training and OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER (q) Incident Command Training
