Texas industrial waste classes: Class 1, 2 and 3

Texas sorts non-hazardous industrial waste into classes. Staff who handle waste need to know them.

Federal RCRA divides waste into hazardous and non-hazardous. Texas goes further for industrial waste: TCEQ sorts non-hazardous industrial waste into classes.

The classes

ClassWhat it is
HazardousMeets the federal or state definition of hazardous waste
Class 1Non-hazardous industrial waste that's still potentially harmful, such as some wastes with certain constituents above limits, or regulated asbestos
Class 2Industrial waste that isn't hazardous, Class 1 or Class 3
Class 3Inert, essentially insoluble wastes like rock, brick and some construction debris

TCEQ's rules (30 TAC Chapter 335) set the exact definitions and tests. Your waste profile should state the class.

Why it matters on the floor

  • Class 1 waste has its own handling, shipping and disposal requirements, including manifesting in Texas.
  • Industrial generators register with TCEQ and get waste codes for each stream.
  • Mixing a hazardous waste into a Class 2 container makes the whole container hazardous.
