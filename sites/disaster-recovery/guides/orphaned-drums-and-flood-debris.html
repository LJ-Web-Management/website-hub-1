Orphaned drums, tanks and cylinders in flood debris

Floods move containers miles from home. What crews should do when they find one.

Floodwater lifts drums, propane tanks, gas cylinders and household chemical containers and leaves them in fields, ditches and debris piles. EPA and state agencies often run dedicated programs to collect them after major floods.

If you find one

  1. Stop and don't touch it. Containers may be damaged, pressurized or leaking.
  2. Look from a distance. Labels, placards, odor, hissing, frost on a cylinder, bulging or leaks.
  3. Mark and report it to your supervisor or the agency running orphan container collection.
  4. Keep moving equipment away from it.

Why they're dangerous

  • Propane and other compressed gas cylinders can leak or rupture.
  • Unknown chemicals can be toxic, corrosive or reactive.
  • Mixed debris can combine incompatible chemicals.
  • Containers weakened by water and impact can fail when lifted.

Who collects them

Collection is HAZWOPER work, done by trained crews with a plan, PPE, monitoring and proper containers for overpacking. If your crew does it, everyone needs HAZWOPER training (OSHA 40 Hour HAZWOPER Training).
