Who in a healthcare facility needs hazardous waste training?

More people than you'd think. A role-by-role guide.

RCRA training rules apply to personnel who handle hazardous waste, not just EHS. In a hospital that's a long list.

Role by role

RoleSuggested training
EHS and waste coordinatorsRCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Training, then RCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Refresher Training annually, plus Hazardous Waste Contingency Plan and Emergency Coordinator Training if you're an LQG
Pharmacists and pharmacy techsRCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Training or Hazardous Waste Satellite Accumulation Area Training, plus Subpart P training where adopted
Lab and histology techsHazardous Waste Satellite Accumulation Area Training
Environmental servicesHazardous Waste Satellite Accumulation Area Training and OSHA 4-Hour HAZWOPER First Responder Awareness Level Training
Facilities, biomed and materials managementRCRA Universal Waste Handler Training
SecurityOSHA 4-Hour HAZWOPER First Responder Awareness Level Training
Anyone who signs a manifestGenerator training plus manifest and DOT training

How often

  • Large quantity generators: within six months of starting the job, and an annual review.
  • Small quantity generators: staff must be thoroughly familiar with handling and emergency procedures. Annual refreshers are a good habit.
  • Universal waste: handlers must inform or train employees on handling and emergency procedures.

Keep records

Inspectors from EPA or your state agency will ask who was trained, when and on what. Certificates filed by role make that a five-minute answer.
