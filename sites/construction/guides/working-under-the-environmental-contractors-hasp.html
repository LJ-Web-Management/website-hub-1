Working under the environmental contractor's health and safety plan

On remediation-driven projects, the site plan often isn't yours. Here's how to work inside it.

On many contaminated-site projects, an environmental consultant or remediation contractor writes the site health and safety plan (HASP) and controls the zones. Your construction crew works inside their system.

Before mobilization

  • Get the HASP and read it, especially hazards, action levels, PPE by task and emergency procedures.
  • Confirm every worker has current training: the 40-hour or 24-hour, a refresher within 12 months, supervisor training for foremen, medical clearance and respirator fit tests if needed.
  • Ask who has stop-work authority. Usually it's both companies' site leads.

On site

  • Attend the daily tailgate. The plan can change daily as monitoring data comes in.
  • Follow the zone boundaries. Construction equipment doesn't cross into the support zone without decon.
  • Report changes, such as new odors, staining, drums or buried debris, right away.

Your responsibilities still apply

Your company still owns its own workers' safety under OSHA's multi-employer citation policy. Following the other company's HASP doesn't replace your own training, PPE program and supervision. That's what OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER Supervisor Training covers.
