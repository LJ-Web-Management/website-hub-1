Where HAZWOPER fits in utility work

Not every leak call is a HAZWOPER response. Here's how utilities usually draw the line.

Utilities have their own federal safety rules (for gas pipelines, DOT's 49 CFR Parts 192 and 195, including operator qualification). HAZWOPER applies alongside them in specific situations.

Usually not HAZWOPER

  • Routine leak surveys and repairs handled under normal operating procedures
  • Incidental releases that the crew can control without significant hazard

Often HAZWOPER

  • Emergency response to a release beyond incidental, for example a large gas release, fire, or liquids spill reaching waterways (1910.120(q))
  • Cleanup of contaminated soil or water after a release, such as a liquids pipeline spill
  • Work in contaminated rights-of-way, for example former manufactured gas plant (MGP) sites

Typical training by role

RoleTraining
Meter readers, locators, inspectorsOSHA 4-Hour HAZWOPER First Responder Awareness Level Training
Field crews who respond to emergenciesOSHA 40 Hour HAZWOPER for Utility/Pipeline Emergency Response and annual refresher
Supervisors and on-call managersOSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER (q) Incident Command Training
Leak investigatorsGas detection: OSHA Air Monitoring and Gas Detection Training

Operator qualification is separate

DOT operator qualification covers specific covered tasks on pipelines. It doesn't replace HAZWOPER, and HAZWOPER doesn't replace it.
