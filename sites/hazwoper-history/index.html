  1. 1976

    RCRA is enacted

    The Resource Conservation and Recovery Act gives EPA authority to control hazardous waste from generation to disposal: "cradle to grave."

  2. 1978

    Love Canal

    Chemical waste buried beneath a Niagara Falls, New York neighborhood leads to a federal emergency declaration and national attention on abandoned waste sites.

  3. 1980

    CERCLA (Superfund)

    Congress passes the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act to clean up uncontrolled hazardous waste sites.

  4. 1984

    RCRA amendments

    The Hazardous and Solid Waste Amendments tighten RCRA, including land disposal restrictions and rules for small quantity generators.

  5. 1986

    SARA Section 126

    The Superfund Amendments and Reauthorization Act directs OSHA to protect workers at hazardous waste sites and in emergency response. OSHA issues an interim final rule in December.

  6. 1989

    HAZWOPER final rule

    OSHA publishes the final Hazardous Waste Operations and Emergency Response standard, 29 CFR 1910.120, on March 6, 1989. EPA publishes 40 CFR Part 311 to cover public employees in states without a state plan.

  7. 1990

    HAZWOPER takes effect

    The final rule becomes effective on March 6, 1990. The same year, the Oil Pollution Act follows the Exxon Valdez spill.

  8. 2010

    Training in a language workers understand

    OSHA states that required training must be presented in a manner, language and vocabulary employees can understand.

  9. 2016

    Generator Improvements Rule

    EPA reorganizes and updates the hazardous waste generator rules, including new requirements like the contingency plan quick reference guide.

  10. 2018

    e-Manifest

    EPA launches the national electronic hazardous waste manifest system.

  11. 2019

    Pharmaceuticals rule

    EPA's hazardous waste pharmaceuticals rule (40 CFR 266 Subpart P) bans sewering of hazardous waste pharmaceuticals by healthcare facilities.
