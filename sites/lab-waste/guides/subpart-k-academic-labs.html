Subpart K: the alternative waste rules for academic labs

Eligible academic institutions can manage lab waste under 40 CFR 262 Subpart K instead of the satellite rules. Here's what changes.

Satellite accumulation rules were written for factories, not teaching labs with hundreds of small containers and students who rotate every semester. EPA's answer is 40 CFR Part 262, Subpart K, an optional set of rules for eligible academic entities.

Who can use it

Colleges and universities, and teaching hospitals and non-profit research institutes formally affiliated with one. Your state must have adopted Subpart K, and the institution must notify EPA or the state that it's opting in.

What changes

TopicSatellite rules (262.15)Subpart K
Who makes the hazardous waste determinationGenerator, at the point of generationA trained professional, and it can be made in the lab, at an on-site central accumulation area or at an on-site TSDF
Labels in the lab"Hazardous Waste" plus hazard indication"Unwanted Material" (or equivalent) plus enough info to identify contents and hazards
Removal from the labWhen 55 gallons is reached (3 days)At least every 12 months, or sooner when volume limits are reached
PlanningNo specific planA written Laboratory Management Plan
Lab clean-outsNo special provisionSpecial allowance for one clean-out per lab every 12 months

Training under Subpart K

Lab workers and students must get training appropriate to their duties, so they understand the rules that apply to them. Trained professionals, typically EHS staff, need RCRA training to make waste determinations.

Is it worth it?

Many universities find Subpart K fits lab life better. But it requires a solid management plan and trained professionals, and it isn't adopted in every state.
