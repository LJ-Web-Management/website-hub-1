Clean agent types: halocarbon vs inert gas

How they work and the hazards of each.

Halocarbon agentsInert gas agents
ExamplesHFC-227ea (FM-200), FK-5-1-12 (Novec 1230)IG-541 (Inergen), IG-55, IG-100 (nitrogen)
How they workMostly absorb heat from the fireLower oxygen to a level that won't support combustion
StorageLiquefied in cylindersHigh-pressure gas, many cylinders
People hazardsDecomposition products in a fire, cold discharge, noiseReduced oxygen, noise, pressure

Common to both

  • Pressurized cylinders that must be secured
  • Room integrity: the room must hold the agent concentration
  • Impairments: when the system is disabled for work, the space needs a fire watch or alternate protection and the right people must be notified

Working in protected rooms

Before work near detectors or that creates dust or heat, follow the site's procedure to place the system in a safe mode, and return it to service afterwards. See Data Center Emergency Power Off (EPO) Procedures Training for how suppression ties into EPO.
