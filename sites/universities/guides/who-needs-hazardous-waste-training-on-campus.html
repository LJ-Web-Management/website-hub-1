Who needs hazardous waste training on a college campus?

A department-by-department guide for campus EHS.

A university can be a large quantity generator, with waste coming from dozens of buildings. Training has to reach well beyond the science departments.

Where campus hazardous waste comes from

DepartmentTypical waste
Chemistry, biology, engineering labsSolvents, reagents, acids, metals
Art and theaterPaints, solvents, photo chemicals, glazes
Facilities and maintenancePaints, adhesives, oils, lamps, batteries
Motor poolUsed oil, antifreeze, solvents, batteries
Athletics and poolsPool chemicals
GroundsPesticides, fuel
ITElectronics and batteries
Medical centerPharmaceuticals and lab waste

Suggested training

RoleCourse
Anyone who fills a waste containerHazardous Waste Satellite Accumulation Area Training
EHS and waste techniciansRCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Training, then RCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Refresher Training yearly
Facilities and ITRCRA Universal Waste Handler Training
Custodians and campus policeOSHA 4-Hour HAZWOPER First Responder Awareness Level Training
Emergency coordinatorsHazardous Waste Contingency Plan and Emergency Coordinator Training

Students

Students in labs and studios generate waste too. Most campuses cover the basics in lab safety orientation and post simple rules at every satellite area.
