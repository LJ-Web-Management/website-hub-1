Chemical storage in warehouses: segregation basics

Storing incompatible chemicals together turns a small leak into a fire or toxic gas. The basics.

Warehouses store everything from pool chemicals to paint to cleaning products, often in the same building. A leak becomes dangerous when incompatible products meet.

Common incompatibles

Keep apartWhy
Oxidizers (pool chlorine, some fertilizers) and flammables or combustiblesFire
Acids and basesHeat and splattering
Bleach and ammonia productsToxic gas
Bleach and acidsChlorine gas
Water-reactive products and anything wetHeat, gas, fire

Practical rules

  1. Read the SDS. Section 7 covers storage and Section 10 covers incompatibilities.
  2. Segregate by hazard class, using separate aisles, distance or barriers.
  3. Flammables belong in rated cabinets or rooms as the fire code requires.
  4. Store liquids low, below solids, and away from rack edges.
  5. Secondary containment for drums and totes.
  6. Keep spill kits near chemical storage, matched to the products.

Your local fire code

Fire codes set limits on how much of each hazard class can be stored in an area. Your fire marshal and insurer will check.
