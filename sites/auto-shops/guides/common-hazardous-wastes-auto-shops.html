Common hazardous wastes at auto repair shops

Where hazardous waste comes from in a typical shop, and how to manage each stream.

Shop waste streams

WasteTypical management
Used oilUsed oil rules: label, recycle
Used oil filtersHot-drain, then recycle
Parts washer solventOften hazardous waste (ignitable or listed). Many shops use a service that replaces solvent
Brake and carb cleaner (aerosol)Spent cans may be universal waste or hazardous waste. Chlorinated cleaners can contaminate other waste
AntifreezeRecycle. Some states regulate it as hazardous if contaminated
Lead-acid batteriesReturn to supplier or recycle
Paint and thinner (body shops)Usually hazardous waste
Shop ragsLaundered by an industrial laundry, or managed as waste
Mercury switches (older vehicles)Universal waste

Biggest mistakes

  1. Pouring solvents into the used oil drum
  2. Open waste containers with funnels left in
  3. Floor drains connected to storm sewers or septic systems. Fluids must never go down these.
  4. No labels

Training

Everyone who fills a waste container should know which drum gets what. Hazardous Waste Satellite Accumulation Area Training for techs, RCRA Hazardous Waste Generator Training for managers and RCRA Universal Waste Handler Training for universal waste.
