Does your facility need an emergency response team, or just an evacuation plan?

Paragraph (q) has an exit. If your people evacuate and never respond, the rules are very different.

Every facility that handles hazardous substances has to decide one thing first: when something releases, do our people respond, or do they leave?

Option 1: evacuate

Under 29 CFR 1910.120(q)(1), employers who evacuate employees from the danger area when an emergency occurs, and don't let them help handle it, are exempt from the HAZWOPER (q) requirements. They must have an emergency action plan that meets 29 CFR 1910.38.

That means:

  • Alarm and reporting procedures
  • Evacuation routes and assembly points
  • A way to account for everyone
  • Training so employees know the plan

You still need awareness-level thinking (recognize a release and report it), and the local fire department or a hazmat contractor does the responding.

Option 2: respond

If any employee will take action on a release beyond incidental cleanup, such as containing it, stopping it or rescuing someone, you're doing emergency response under (q). You'll need:

  • A written emergency response plan under (q)(1) and (q)(2)
  • Responders trained to the level of their role
  • An incident command system
  • PPE, monitoring equipment and decon
  • Medical surveillance for hazmat team members
  • Annual refresher training or demonstrated competency

How to choose

QuestionLeans evacuateLeans respond
How fast can the fire department arrive?MinutesFar away or slow
Can a release shut down your process or hurt neighbors before help arrives?UnlikelyLikely
Do you have staff who can train and stay current?NoYes
Do you have the PPE and equipment?NoYes

A middle path

Many plants evacuate most staff but keep a trained in-plant ERT at the operations level. The ERT protects people and contains from a distance until the fire department takes over. That's a (q) program, but a focused one. See Manufacturing Emergency Response Team (ERT) Training.
