Radiological awareness on cleanup sites

Some federal and industrial sites have radiological contamination. What HAZWOPER workers should know.

HAZWOPER covers chemical, physical, biological and radiological hazards. On some federal sites, especially in the DOE weapons complex, radiological contamination is part of the job.

Basic protection principles

  • Time: spend as little time near sources as possible
  • Distance: dose drops quickly with distance
  • Shielding: put material between you and the source
  • Contamination control: keep radioactive material off skin and out of lungs

On site, expect

  • Rad worker training on top of HAZWOPER, required at DOE sites under 10 CFR 835
  • Dosimetry to track exposure
  • Radiological control technicians surveying work areas, equipment and people
  • Step-off pads and frisking when leaving contaminated areas
  • Contamination area postings that you must obey

Where it overlaps HAZWOPER

The site safety plan, PPE, monitoring and decon all address radiological hazards alongside chemical ones. Your 40-hour and refresher (OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER Refresher Training) provide the base, and site-specific rad training builds on it.
