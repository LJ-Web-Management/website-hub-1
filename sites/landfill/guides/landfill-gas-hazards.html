Landfill gas: methane, H2S and oxygen

Decomposing waste makes gas that can explode, suffocate or poison. What landfill staff need to know.

Decomposing waste produces landfill gas, mostly methane and carbon dioxide, with smaller amounts of hydrogen sulfide and other compounds.

The hazards

GasHazard
MethaneFlammable between about 5% and 15% in air. Can collect in structures, vaults and manholes
Carbon dioxideHeavier than air, displaces oxygen in low areas
Hydrogen sulfideToxic, deadens sense of smell
Low oxygenAny confined space connected to the gas system

Where it collects

  • Gas collection wells, headers and condensate sumps
  • Leachate manholes and pump stations
  • Buildings on or near the waste footprint, especially basements
  • Utility trenches crossing the landfill

Working safely

  • Monitor before entering any vault, manhole or trench: oxygen first, then LEL, then toxics.
  • Treat gas system structures as potential permit-required confined spaces.
  • No ignition sources near wellheads and flares.
  • Ventilate and keep monitoring during work.
